If you did not heed my advice in my last post and buy a used Thinkpad, then I
highly suggest you reconsider.

<!-- more -->

All of my previous reasoning remains true, but the part about repairability
should be taken at double the value after what I'm about to share with you.

## Buying a replacement

I ended my last post by discussing the fate of my used Thinkpad. In short, my
water bottle leaked in my backpack on a trip and soaked my laptop. Now it won't
turn on. Luckily, I was pretty sure the RAM and SSD, probably worth more than
the rest of the laptop combined, were undamaged, so I was able to look for used
Thinkpads that already had these components stripped from them. These machines
varied wildly in price, but I was willing to pay a higher price for a
high-quality chassis and better CPU. In the end, I found a $40 model with a
resonably good chassis and a CPU one generation newer.

## The repair

The laptop took a while to arrive, but when it did I immediately began migrating
the RAM and SSD to the new computer. I turned it on, praying that it would
actually work and that I hadn't thrown $40 away completely. To my immense
happiness, it did boot, although entering the BIOS, I noticed something off: the
interface seemed bigger. I attributed it to a different BIOS version and changed
the settings to match what I had on the prior motherboard. However, after
logging into the system, the problem became apparent. The screen had a
significantly lower quality than previously.

I had two options: either live with a terrible display with less than Full HD
resolution, or swap the display over to my new device in addition to the RAM and
SSD. After deciding that replacing the screen would be too difficult, I decided
to just replace the entire top half of the laptop, which went surprisingly
smoothly. After booting into the system again to verify I hadn't broken
anything, I called the repair finished.

## The aftermath

In the end, I had a laptop that worked as good as new, as well as a gutted top
and bottom half that I still need to figure out what to do with. The repair was
a complete success. I have had some minor issues, like some of the clips in the
case breaking during the swap, leading to the bottom cover separating from the
rest of the laptop in the back, but not enough to make me concerned.

### Fingerprint reader

Another thing I realized once I got the replacement laptop was that it had a
fingerprint reader. I was pretty excited, until I tried to actually use it. It
turns out that the specific fingerprint reader used on the Thinkpad T470 is not
supported by fprintd, and the third-party driver for it is broken and has not
been updated in five years.

However, this did not take away any functionality that I previously had, so I
wasn't very disappointed.

### Was it worth it?

Absolutely.

The repair was worth every last penny of the $40 I spent on it. Not only do I
still have a functional laptop and a bunch of spare parts, but I also saved an
object with sentimental value and brushed up on my laptop repair skills. This
laptop means so much more to me now that I've brought it back from death. I will
continue to use this laptop until it stops working and there is nothing that I
can do about it.

In other words, I will keep using this laptop forever. Maybe.
